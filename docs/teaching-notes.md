# Teaching notes

Practical things that are not on a slide.

## The two decks

Every module ships a **presenter** and a **student** copy. The presenter copy contains
hidden pages for the segments drawn live on the iPad: numbered drawing steps on the left,
the finished panel on the right so you know what yours should look like, and the script in
the speaker notes. The student copy has none of that.

Share the student copy. The hidden pages give away the drawing before you draw it, and the
production marks mean nothing to a learner.

## The iPad segments

Each hidden page is one new iPad page. Draw it live rather than showing the finished
panel — the drawing is the pacing. The finished panel exists so you can check yourself,
and so the deck is still usable if somebody else teaches the session.

## The one dangerous command

`--no-caps` in the capstone is the only genuinely risky thing in the course, and only on
the looping input. Keep the step ceiling on, run it once to prove the alert fires, and turn
the caps back on before you walk away from the machine.

Say that out loud when you demo it. People copy what they see.

## Pacing

Runtimes on the section dividers are what the sessions actually take when the live drawing
happens at a normal speed. If you are running long, the appendices are cuttable and the
iPad segments are not — the drawing is where the ideas land.

## What to cut when you are short

Cut appendix B (glossary) and appendix C (commands) from the delivered session; they exist
so nobody has to scrub back through the video. Never cut the "turn it off" demonstrations —
they are the only part that changes anybody's mind.
